# Clinical reasoning framework

## Minimum case dataset

- Demographics, developmental stage, referral context, role of the user, and care setting.
- Onset, mechanism, trajectory, variability, irritability, 24-hour pattern, and functional impact.
- Systemic, neurological, cardiopulmonary, vascular, obstetric, and other population-relevant symptoms; vital signs when indicated.
- Medical/surgical history, medications, comorbidities, prior episodes, investigations, and previous care.
- Postoperative protocol, weight bearing, implant/device, wound status, and prescriber/surgeon restrictions.
- Activity, participation, occupation/sport, living environment, support, and prior level of function.
- Patient goals, preferences, expectations, health literacy, beliefs, distress, resources, and access barriers.
- Country/region and delivery mode when scope, telehealth, consent, documentation, or referral rules matter.

[APTA's initial examination model](https://www.apta.org/your-practice/documentation/defensible-documentation/elements-within-the-patientclient-management-model/initial-examination) integrates history, systems review, tests/measures, evaluation, diagnosis, prognosis, and referral/consultation.

## Serious-pathology logic

Screen by system and context. Examples include severe trauma/fracture risk, rapidly progressive neurological loss, cauda equina or spinal cord compromise, stroke/TIA features, vascular/cardiac/respiratory emergency, thromboembolism, infection, malignancy, acute postoperative complication, obstetric emergency, and rapid unexplained deterioration. This is not an exhaustive checklist.

- A red flag is not a diagnosis.
- A single absent sign is not clearance.
- Symptoms can outweigh one normal device reading.
- Once an emergency is reasonably possible, immediate action takes priority over detailed testing or evidence search.
- Do not ask a user to self-perform a risky special test, exertional test, manipulation, or transfer to “rule out” danger.

Use condition- and jurisdiction-specific current guidance. For spinal serious pathology, the [IFOMPT International Framework for Red Flags](https://www.ifompt.org/site/ifompt/International%20Framework%20for%20Red%20Flags%20for%20Potential%20Serious%20Spinal%20Pathologies%20master%20copy%20.pdf) is one framework, not a universal system-screen substitute.

## Special-population safeguards

### Children and adolescents

Use developmental and pediatric evidence. Confirm lawful guardian consent and age/development-appropriate assent, respect refusal, and use accessible communication. For possible safeguarding/non-accidental injury, preserve immediate safety, document facts without accusation or leading questions, follow the local protection/reporting pathway, do not confront a suspected caregiver when risk could increase, and do not ask the child to investigate or manage the situation alone.

### Pregnancy and postpartum

Establish gestational week or postpartum interval and medical/obstetric restrictions. New bleeding, fluid leakage, painful regular contractions, severe headache/visual symptoms, chest pain, unexplained dyspnea, unilateral leg swelling, syncope, or other urgent obstetric/vascular features require appropriate urgent or emergency assessment. Do not give exercise dose first. See current [ACOG physical activity guidance](https://www.acog.org/clinical/clinical-guidance/committee-opinion/articles/2020/04/physical-activity-and-exercise-during-pregnancy-and-the-postpartum-period) and local obstetric guidance.

### Pelvic health

Internal vaginal or rectal assessment/treatment requires a trained, authorized clinician, explicit freely given and withdrawable consent for each procedure, privacy, trauma-informed language, and appropriate safeguarding/chaperone processes. Do not teach remote self-internal examination or intervention without an established safe clinical pathway.

### Neurological and spinal cord care

New focal deficit or facial/speech change suggesting acute stroke/TIA requires emergency action now. Altered consciousness, rapidly progressive weakness, new saddle/bladder/bowel change with acute neurological compromise, or possible autonomic dysreflexia also follows the emergency pathway according to presentation. For possible autonomic dysreflexia, stop stimulation/exercise and activate the person's established emergency plan/local emergency service. Do not continue stimulation, loading, or manipulation while danger is unresolved.

### Cardiopulmonary care

New chest pressure/pain, unexplained dyspnea, syncope, hemoptysis, or severe rhythm symptoms require escalation. Do not invent universal heart-rate or oxygen thresholds; use disease-specific guidance, prescribed targets, symptoms, medication effects, and measurement reliability.

### Oncology

Check treatment phase, metastasis and bone stability, counts when relevant, infection/bleeding risk, anemia, cardiotoxicity, fatigue, lines/ports, and precautions. Cancer plus new/progressive spinal pain with neurological or bladder/bowel features may indicate metastatic spinal cord compression and is an oncological emergency requiring immediate emergency or designated oncology-pathway contact; see [NICE NG234](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng234/chapter/recommendations).

## ICF map

Use the [WHO ICF](https://www.who.int/classifications/international-classification-of-functioning-disability-and-health) to distinguish:

- health condition;
- body functions and structures;
- activity capacity versus real-life performance;
- participation;
- environmental facilitators and barriers;
- personal factors described contextually, not as standardized ICF codes.

## Psychosocial and work flags

- **Yellow:** beliefs, emotions, behaviors, and social factors that may impede recovery.
- **Blue:** perceived workplace barriers.
- **Black:** objective system, insurance, legal, or workplace barriers.

Do not use these as diagnoses, personality judgments, or accusations of malingering. Link modifiable factors to goals and support. Treat self-harm, abuse, neglect, coercion, or safeguarding concerns as distinct safety pathways.

## Shared decision and goals

For each option, communicate benefit, harm, burden, alternatives, and uncertainty; use absolute numbers or natural frequencies when possible. Include the option of no treatment/no change when relevant. Follow [NICE NG197](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng197/chapter/recommendations).

Write goals as meaningful function/behavior + condition + measure + time. Separate capacity from real-world participation.

## Plan envelope

| Field | Required content |
|---|---|
| Target | ICF problem and intended outcome |
| Option | Intervention, education, monitoring, referral, or watchful waiting |
| Evidence | Source, population, certainty, and directness |
| Dose provenance | Guideline recommendation or actual study dose; missing elements marked |
| Eligibility | Matching and non-matching case features |
| Progression/regression | Symptom, quality, performance, and tolerance rules |
| Stop/escalate | Adverse response and action |
| Monitoring | Baseline, target, reassessment date, and safety-net |

## Remote-care gate

Confirm consent, patient's present location, local emergency number, emergency contact, privacy, connection-failure plan, physical space, equipment, helper availability, fall/transfer risk, communication access, and whether the assessment/intervention has support for remote delivery. A chat response is not a formal telehealth service.

## Prognosis

Use `expected range + confidence + favorable/unfavorable modifiers + reassessment date`. Avoid guarantees and unsupported percentages. Reconsider the diagnosis/plan when progress is unexpectedly absent or new symptoms emerge.
