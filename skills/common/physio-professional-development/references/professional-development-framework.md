# Professional development and practice-improvement framework

## Competency anchors

- [WHO Rehabilitation Competency Framework](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789240008281) organizes rehabilitation work across Practice, Professionalism, Learning and Development, Research, and Management and Leadership.
- [World Physiotherapy Standards of Physiotherapist Practice](https://world.physio/guideline/standards) describes safe and effective competence in context.
- [World Physiotherapy Education Framework](https://world.physio/what-we-do/education/physiotherapist-education-framework) supports entry-level competence and self-directed continuing professional development.
- [World Physiotherapy ethical principles](https://world.physio/policy/ps-ethical-responsibilities-and-principles) provides an ethical anchor; local code and law govern the actual practitioner.

Use these as adaptable global reference frameworks, not a replacement for local standards.

## Competency evidence map

| Domain | Useful evidence |
|---|---|
| Practice | Case reasoning, observed skill, outcome use, safety/referral decisions |
| Professionalism | Consent, privacy, ethics, scope, accountability, equity |
| Communication/collaboration | Patient feedback, teach-back, handoff, team observation |
| Evidence/research | Search, appraisal, implementation, audit/research contribution |
| Learning/teaching | Needs analysis, feedback, supervision, teaching evaluation |
| Quality | Audit, adverse-event learning, process/outcome improvement |
| Management/leadership | Prioritization, service design, resources, team development |

Confidence and course attendance are weak evidence by themselves.

## CPD cycle

1. **Need:** What gap or service problem matters, and what evidence shows it?
2. **Objective:** What observable behavior or result should change, by when?
3. **Activity:** Which learning method best fits the gap?
4. **Quality check:** Is the source current, evidence-based, transparent, accessible, and free from misleading commercial claims?
5. **Practice:** Where will the skill be applied under appropriate supervision?
6. **Feedback:** Who or what will assess performance?
7. **Impact:** What changed in knowledge, skill, behavior, patient/service outcome, or safety?
8. **Reflection:** What worked, what did not, why, and what changes next?
9. **Record:** Save source, activity, evidence, result, and follow-up without inventing credits.

## Journal club

Use this structure:

- clinical question and why it matters;
- study design and actual PICO;
- key methods and design-specific bias;
- effect with uncertainty and clinical importance;
- harms and excluded populations;
- consistency with current guidelines/syntheses;
- applicability to local patients/resources;
- one practice decision, one uncertainty, one follow-up action.

Do not let presentation quality substitute for appraisal.

## Reflective practice

Use a non-punitive format:

1. situation and intended goal;
2. information available at the time;
3. decision and action;
4. outcome and feedback;
5. cognitive, communication, system, or resource contributors;
6. evidence or supervision needed;
7. specific behavior/system change;
8. review date.

Remove patient identifiers.

## Quality improvement

Distinguish outcome, process, structure, and balancing measures. Define numerator, denominator, inclusion/exclusion, source, frequency, target, and unintended consequences. Start with a small test of change, review data, adapt, and plan sustainability. Check whether the activity is clinical audit, service evaluation, QI, or research under local governance.

## Course and technology appraisal

Check provider, faculty competence, conflicts, evidence date, learning outcomes, practice/feedback, assessment, accessibility, data/privacy, device claims, and transfer to local scope. Marketing labels, CE/CPD credits, and certificates must be verified with the issuing regulator/provider.

## Literature-watch template

- topic and structured question;
- sources and full queries;
- cadence and last-search date;
- inclusion/exclusion and alert threshold;
- deduplication and version/retraction check;
- appraisal and certainty method;
- old-versus-new evidence comparison;
- concise output: what changed, confidence, practice impact, and next action.

## Research, thesis, and protocol gate

When the task goes beyond finding/appraising evidence and asks to design or conduct research:

1. define the research question, rationale, stakeholder/patient involvement, design, setting, eligibility, intervention/exposure/comparator, outcomes/time points, analysis estimand, and feasibility;
2. distinguish research from audit, service evaluation, and quality improvement under current local governance;
3. require appropriate ethics review/approval or documented exemption before recruitment or data collection—never fabricate approval;
4. plan consent/assent, capacity, safeguarding, adverse events, stopping rules, compensation, conflicts, protocol deviations, and participant burden;
5. preregister prospectively when applicable; use an appropriate public registry and versioned protocol/statistical analysis plan;
6. justify sample size from the design and target estimate, not a generic rule; involve a qualified methodologist/statistician for complex designs;
7. define data minimisation, coding/pseudonymisation, access, retention, transfer, security, quality checks, missing data, and reproducible analysis;
8. select the relevant reporting guideline through EQUATOR, but do not confuse reporting completeness with bias control;
9. plan dissemination of results, harms, null findings, protocol/code/data availability, and accessible participant feedback.

The skill may draft a protocol scaffold. It must not act as an ethics committee, sponsor, data controller, trial registry, statistical guarantee, or legal determination.
